CHALLENGE: DOO Builders League, "Beyond the Chatbot". RollCue is a public demo web app: anonymous visitors upload PDF, PNG or JPEG print files with free-text order notes, RollCue inspects them in a worker thread, builds production PDFs, and a human approves a release. It will be hosted publicly on a free container (one instance, Postgres in production, PGlite locally). It never prints or controls hardware.

THIS REVIEW: R6, security. Can a stranger on the internet harm the service, other visitors, or the host, or make RollCue claim or do something it should not?

Read what you need: src/http.ts (routing, sessions, cookies, CSRF header, origin checks, rate limits, proxy hops, body limits, response headers, static files), src/main.ts (config, env, test hooks), src/service.ts (session scoping of every query, quotas, retention, metrics), src/db.ts, src/inspect.ts, src/worker.ts, src/pool.ts, src/pdfout.ts (hostile-file handling, resource limits), ui/src (rendering of untrusted filenames and notes, downloads), Dockerfile, render.yaml or deploy files if present, .github/workflows, package.json and the lockfile, .gitignore, docs/SECURITY.md, docs/DEPLOY.md. Commits f23555e and 2817869 fixed earlier approval and recovery findings, including a test-only hook `/api/test/slow-next-prepare`; check that test hooks cannot be enabled in production.

YOUR TASKS:
1. **Access control.** Can one session read, download, approve, change or learn anything about another session's jobs, proposals, artifacts, previews, events, corrections or metrics? Guessable IDs, missing session filters, timing or error-message oracles, the legacy surface.
2. **Request forgery and browser security.** Is the custom-header CSRF defence sound for every mutating route (including uploads and test hooks)? Cookie flags, CORS, CSP, framing, content types and `Content-Disposition` on downloads, XSS through filenames, notes, PDF metadata or SVG. Anything served from a user-controlled path.
3. **Hostile files.** Decompression bombs, deep nesting, huge declared dimensions, malformed xref, many pages, embedded JavaScript or actions, font tricks, JPEG/PNG chunk abuse. Can one upload exhaust memory, CPU, disk or the worker for everyone? Are the documented caps enforced before the expensive work?
4. **Abuse of a public demo.** Rate limits and their keying behind a proxy (`TRUST_PROXY_HOPS`, `X-Forwarded-For` spoofing), unbounded tables (sessions, events, metrics, corrections), session creation without limit, retention and purge, request smuggling or slowloris-style body handling, log injection.
5. **Configuration and supply chain.** Test hooks, debug output, stack traces in responses, secrets in the repo or its history (scan tracked files and `git log -p` for keys, tokens, private paths), the Docker image (user, layers, what is copied), CI permissions and pinned actions, dependency risk.
6. **Integrity claims.** Can a visitor forge or alter the audit chain, a ticket, a binding hash or a verification result, or make the UI say "released" or "verified" when it is not?
7. Any claim in docs/SECURITY.md or docs/DEPLOY.md that the code does not support.

FORMAT: plain markdown, at most about 1500 words. Number each finding R6-1, R6-2, … with a severity (blocker / major / minor / nit), evidence (file:line or a concrete request sequence), and the smallest fix. For each reproducible defect, describe a concrete failing test. Do not include working exploit payloads beyond what a regression test needs. End with a one-paragraph verdict: is RollCue safe to expose publicly as a free demo?
